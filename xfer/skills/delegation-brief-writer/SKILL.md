# Delegation Brief Writer

Most delegation fails because the brief lives in the user's head. A good brief answers every question the other person would ask, shows what good looks like, and builds in a check before too much time is spent.

## What to collect

1. The task and why it matters.
2. Who will do it and their experience level.
3. What the finished result looks like (examples, references, past work).
4. Tools, files, logins and access needed.
5. Deadline, budget and payment method.
6. Things that went wrong in past delegation.

## The brief structure

1. **Context:** the business, the client or audience, why this task exists.
2. **The goal:** what success looks like in one sentence.
3. **Deliverables:** exact outputs, formats, sizes, lengths, quantities, file names, where to upload.
4. **References:** examples to copy (and why), examples to avoid.
5. **Instructions:** numbered steps for anything specific to the user's way of working.
6. **Style rules:** brand fonts, colours, tone, captions, music, logo placement.
7. **Tools and access:** what they'll use, how access is shared securely.
8. **Timeline:** first draft date (a small sample first for new people), final date, check-in points.
9. **Quality checklist:** what they should check before submitting.
10. **When stuck:** how and when to ask questions, response times.
11. **Payment:** amount, when, how, revisions included.

## Rules

- Assume no context. Spell out the obvious.
- Show, don't only tell: references and examples.
- Ask for a small sample first with new people.
- Secure access (collaborator roles, password managers), never passwords in chat.

## Output format

```
TASK BRIEF: [Title]
For: ... | Due: ...

1. Context
...
11. Payment

REPLY WITH
"Got it" plus any questions before you start.
```

## Final check

- [ ] Clear goal and deliverables
- [ ] References included
- [ ] Sample or check-in planned
- [ ] Quality checklist
