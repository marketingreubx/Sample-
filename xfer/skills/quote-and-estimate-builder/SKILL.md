# Quote and Estimate Builder

A quote should make the price feel logical and the scope unmistakable. Clients argue about prices when they do not understand what is included or when surprises appear later. This skill makes both impossible.

## What to collect

1. The project description and the client's goal.
2. Deliverables and quantities (or enough detail to estimate them).
3. The user's rate or package prices.
4. Timeline, deadlines and any rush.
5. Payment preferences (deposit, milestones, monthly).
6. Currency, tax or VAT rules if any.

## The method

### 1. Break down the work
List each deliverable or phase as a line item. For each: description, quantity, unit (hours, items, posts, pages, days), rate, subtotal. Group into phases if long.

### 2. Estimate honestly
For time based items, estimate optimistic, likely and pessimistic hours, then price on the likely number plus a 15% to 25% buffer for revisions and communication. Show the buffer as "project management and revisions", not hidden padding.

### 3. Assumptions
What the price depends on: content supplied by the client, number of revision rounds, response times, access to accounts, brand assets ready.

### 4. Exclusions
What is not included: ad spend, stock photos, printing, extra revisions, new features, translations.

### 5. Optional add ons
2 to 4 useful extras with prices (rush delivery, extra revisions, training session, monthly maintenance).

### 6. Terms
Deposit (for example 50% upfront), milestone payments, due dates, late payment note, validity (for example 14 days), what happens if the scope changes (change request at the hourly rate).

### 7. Summary line
One sentence that ties the total to the outcome: "Total: 450,000 for a booking-ready website live in 3 weeks."

## Rules

- Every line has a clear description a non-expert understands.
- Totals must add up. Double check the math.
- Taxes shown separately if they apply.
- Do not undervalue by hiding work. Show it.
