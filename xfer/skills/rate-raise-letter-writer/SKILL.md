# Rate Raise Letter Writer

Raising prices is normal business. Clients accept it when the message is early, clear, grateful and gives them time. This skill plans the increase, writes the message and prepares the user for every reaction.

## What to collect

1. Current price and new price for each client (or the percentage).
2. The reasons: more experience, better results, more demand, rising costs, expanded service.
3. Relationship details: how long each client has worked with the user, results achieved together.
4. Notice period the user can give (30 to 60 days is typical).
5. Whether the user is willing to keep some clients at a legacy rate or offer a transition.

## The plan

1. **New clients first:** new pricing applies to all new inquiries immediately.
2. **Existing clients:** give 30 to 60 days' notice before the new rate starts.
3. **Size:** 10% to 25% is common for existing clients. Bigger jumps may need a phased approach or added value.
4. **Order:** tell your best long-term clients personally (call or voice) before others.
5. **Options:** keep the price with reduced scope, a longer commitment at a smaller increase, or a phased increase.

## The message

1. Thank them with a specific memory or result.
2. State the change clearly in the first half: new rate and start date.
3. One or two lines on why (keep it short, no long apology).
4. What stays the same or improves.
5. Options if relevant.
6. An invitation to talk.

Tone: confident, warm, brief. No over-explaining, no apologising.

## Replies to pushback

- "That's a big jump." Acknowledge, restate value, offer a phased increase or scope option.
- "Can you keep me at the old rate?" Decide in advance. If yes, set a date. If no, kindly hold firm.
- "We'll need to find someone else." Thank them, offer a smooth handover, leave the door open.
- No reply: follow up after 5 days.

## Rules

- Never raise prices in the middle of a delivery problem.
- Never apologise for the increase or hide it in an invoice.
- Put the new rate and date in writing.
- Be ready to lose a client or two. That is part of the plan.

