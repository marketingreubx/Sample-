# Invoice and Follow Up

Late payment is usually disorganisation, not bad intent. Clear invoices, friendly reminders on a schedule and fair terms get most clients to pay on time while keeping the relationship. When they do not, the messages escalate calmly and in writing.

## What to collect

1. Client name, invoice number, amount, currency, due date, what it covers.
2. Payment methods (bank transfer details, payment link).
3. Days overdue (if any) and history with this client.
4. Current terms (deposit, net 7, net 14, late fee) and whether work is ongoing.
5. Channel: email, WhatsApp, both.

## The sequence

- **Sending the invoice:** short, friendly, amount, due date, what it covers, how to pay, thank you.
- **3 days before due:** gentle heads-up with the payment link.
- **On the due date:** "Just a reminder that invoice #... is due today."
- **3 days late:** assume an oversight, resend details, ask if anything is needed (a PO, a different method).
- **7 days late:** firmer, state the amount and days overdue, ask for a payment date.
- **14 days late:** mention the late fee (only if in the terms) and that work will pause on a specific date if unpaid.
- **30 days late:** final notice in writing, next steps (pause, collections, small claims), still polite.
- **Pause of work message:** factual, no emotion, how to resume.
- **Payment received:** thank you.

Each message gets an email and a shorter WhatsApp version.

## Better terms for the future

- 50% deposit before work starts, or full payment upfront for small projects.
- Monthly retainers paid in advance.
- Net 7 instead of net 30 for small clients.
- Late fee clause (for example 5% after 14 days), where allowed by law.
- Work and file delivery tied to payment.
- Automatic reminders in the invoicing tool.

## Rules

- Calm, factual, never emotional or insulting.
- Always include the amount, invoice number, due date and how to pay.
- Only mention late fees or consequences that are in the agreement.
- Keep a written record. Phone calls should be followed by a written summary.
- Suggest a lawyer or local small claims process for serious cases.
