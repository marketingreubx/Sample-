# Cold Email Writer

A cold email has one job: earn a reply. Not a sale, not a call, a reply. Every line must make the reader think "this person actually looked at my business" and every word that does not serve that gets cut. The standard is an email a busy owner reads in 15 seconds on a phone and answers.

## What to collect first

Ask in one short message for anything missing. If the user wants speed, assume and label the assumption.

1. **Sender:** name, what they do, who they help, the result they get, one real proof point (client result, number, recognisable client). No proof yet is fine, say so.
2. **Prospect:** name, business, website or profile, and anything specific the user noticed (a gap, a launch, a review, a hiring post, a slow site, no booking link).
3. **Offer for this email:** what the user wants after the reply (a call, sending a free audit, a quick video, a sample).
4. **Channel details:** sending from a personal inbox or a tool, and whether this is the first email or a follow up.

If the user has no observation about the prospect, stop and tell them to find one. Offer three places to look: their website's booking path, their last 9 posts, their Google reviews. A cold email without a real observation is spam.

## The method

### 1. Pick the angle (one only)
- **Problem spotted:** a visible gap that costs them money. Strongest default.
- **Result for a peer:** the user got a measurable result for a similar business.
- **Trigger event:** they just launched, hired, opened, rebranded, got press.
- **Free value first:** offer a mini audit, a short video, 3 ideas.
- **Curiosity question:** one easy question about their goals. Lowest pressure, best for senior people.

### 2. Write the subject line
- 2 to 6 words, lowercase, looks like a colleague wrote it.
- Reference them, not you: "quick question about glow studio", "your booking page", "idea for {business}".
- Never: "Partnership opportunity", "Grow your business", all caps, emojis, fake "Re:".

### 3. Write the body (50 to 110 words)
1. **Opening line about them**, specific and true. Not "I hope this finds you well", not "I came across your page".
2. **The observation and what it costs**, in plain words.
3. **Who you are in one line** with proof if real: "I help skincare clinics fill their books through Instagram. One clinic went from 6 to 21 bookings a month."
4. **One easy ask**, a yes or no question: "Want me to send 3 quick fixes?" Not "Do you have 30 minutes this week?"
5. **Sign off** with first name only. Optional one line with a link only if it helps.

### 4. Write the follow ups
Reply in the same thread so the first email is visible below.
- **Day 3:** two lines, add one new detail or idea. "One more thing I noticed..."
- **Day 7:** a mini case study or proof in 3 sentences.
- **Day 14:** pure value, one tip they can use today, no ask beyond "happy to share more".
- **Day 30:** the polite close. "I will stop here. If {result} becomes a priority, just reply and I will pick it up." This one gets surprising replies.

## Rules

- Write like a person: contractions, short sentences, no corporate words (leverage, synergy, solutions, elevate, unlock, game changer).
- One idea per email. One link maximum. No attachments on the first email.
- Never invent results, client names or numbers. If there is no proof, lean on the observation and a free offer.
- Do not compliment generically. Compliments must be specific and true.
- The prospect should be able to reply with one word.
- Personal first names in the greeting when known. "Hi there" only when there is no name.
- Mobile first: no paragraph longer than 3 lines on a phone.
- Deliverability: send from a real inbox, plain text, no tracking pixels on early emails if possible, 20 to 40 cold emails a day per inbox while warming.

## Output format

```
SUBJECT OPTIONS
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...

EMAIL 1 (send now)
...

FOLLOW UP 1 (day 3, reply in thread)
...

FOLLOW UP 2 (day 7)
...

FOLLOW UP 3 (day 14)
...

FOLLOW UP 4 (day 30)
...

WHY THIS SHOULD WORK
- The observation used and why it matters to them
- What to personalise before sending (bracketed parts)
```

Mark every detail the user must confirm with [square brackets].

## Example

Subject: your booking page

Hi Amaka,

I tried to book a facial on glowbyamaka.com and the only option was to call during work hours. Most of your Instagram traffic comes in at night, so a lot of those people never book.

I help skincare studios turn Instagram followers into bookings. One studio went from 6 to 21 bookings a month after we added a simple booking page.

Want me to send a 2 minute video showing how I would fix yours?

Zainab

## Final check

- [ ] Subject under 7 words, no hype
- [ ] First line is about them and is true
- [ ] Under 110 words, readable on a phone
- [ ] One ask, answerable in one word
- [ ] No invented proof, no buzzwords, no em dashes
- [ ] Follow ups each add something new
