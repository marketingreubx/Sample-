# Value-Based Pricing Pitch

A price only feels expensive when it is compared to nothing. This skill compares it to the cost of the client's problem and the value of the result, using numbers the client gives, so they do the math themselves and the price makes sense.

## What to collect

1. The client's business numbers or estimates: average sale or booking value, monthly customers, conversion rates, hours spent, current costs, lost revenue.
2. The result the user's service creates (more bookings, saved hours, fewer refunds, faster launches).
3. The proposed price and payment options.
4. Proof from similar clients.

If numbers are missing, give the user questions to ask on the call to get them.

## The method

### 1. Questions to get the numbers
- "What's a new client worth to you on average, over a year?"
- "How many new clients do you get a month now?"
- "How many hours a week do you spend on this?"
- "What does an hour of your time make you?"
- "What happens if this stays the same for another 6 months?"

### 2. Calculate the cost of the problem
Lost revenue per month, wasted hours × hourly value, missed opportunities. Show conservative numbers.

### 3. Calculate the value of the result
Conservative, likely and strong scenarios. Example: 5 extra bookings a month × 30,000 = 150,000 a month = 1.8 million a year.

### 4. Compare to the price
Return multiple, payback period ("pays for itself after 2 extra bookings"), price per day.

### 5. Write the pitch
- On a call: recap their numbers, ask them to confirm, then show the comparison and stop talking.
- In a proposal: an "investment and return" box.
- In DMs: one or two lines.

## Rules

- Use the client's numbers, confirmed by them. Never inflate.
- Use conservative scenarios. Under-promise.
- Never guarantee the return unless the user is offering a guarantee.
- Keep the math simple enough to do in their head.

## Output format

```
QUESTIONS TO ASK
...

THE MATH
Cost of the problem: ...
Value of the result (conservative / likely / strong): ...
Price: ...
Return: ... | Pays for itself after: ...

CALL PITCH
...

PROPOSAL BOX
...

DM VERSION
...
```

## Final check

- [ ] Numbers come from the client
- [ ] Conservative scenario leads
- [ ] Pitch lets the client draw the conclusion
- [ ] No guaranteed returns
