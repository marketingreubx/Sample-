# Competitor Analyzer

The goal is not a pretty table. The goal is one decision: how should the user position and price so that the right buyer chooses them without comparing on price. Every finding must lead to an action.

## What to collect

1. The user's business: offer, price, audience, links.
2. 3 to 5 competitors: names and links (website, Instagram, Google listing, marketplace profile).
3. What the user believes makes them different (to be tested).
4. Their goal: more leads, higher prices, a new niche, a launch.

If the user cannot browse, ask them to paste each competitor's homepage text, bio, pricing and 10 recent reviews. Only analyse what is provided or visible. Never invent competitor data.

## The method

### 1. Snapshot each competitor
For each: who they target, main promise, offer and packages, price (or price signals), proof (reviews, results, logos), content style and frequency, funnel (how a stranger becomes a client), and one thing they do very well.

### 2. Read the reviews
Pull out praise themes and complaint themes. Complaints are gold: they are promises the user can make. Quote short phrases.

### 3. Map the market
Place everyone on two axes that matter to buyers in this market (for example: price low to high, and generalist to specialist; or done-for-you to DIY). Find the empty or weak corner.

### 4. Find the gaps
- Audiences nobody speaks to directly.
- Problems mentioned in complaints that nobody promises to fix.
- Proof nobody shows (before and after, numbers, process).
- Formats nobody offers (fixed price, speed, guarantee, payment plan, retainers).
- Channels nobody uses well.

### 5. Recommend
- **Positioning statement:** "For [who] who [situation], [business] is the [category] that [specific promise], unlike [alternative] which [weakness]."
- **3 offer changes** (package, price frame, guarantee, bonus).
- **5 messaging lines** for bio, website hero and DMs.
- **3 cheap tests** with a success metric and a deadline.

## Rules

- Facts first, opinion second. Label anything inferred.
- No copying competitors' words or designs. Use them to find a different place to stand.
- Do not recommend competing on lowest price unless the user has a real cost advantage.
- Be honest when a competitor is clearly stronger, and say where the user can still win.
