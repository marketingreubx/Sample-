# Business Idea Validator

The only real validation is people paying, or clearly committing to pay. Compliments, likes and "that's a great idea" are not evidence. This skill finds the assumption most likely to kill the idea and designs the cheapest fast test of it, with a decision rule set in advance.

## What to collect

1. The idea in the user's words.
2. Who it's for and the problem it solves.
3. Price idea and how it would be delivered.
4. The user's resources: time, money, skills, audience.
5. Any evidence so far.

## The method

### 1. Sharpen
One sentence: "[Offer] helps [specific customer] [get result] for [price]." Rewrite until specific.

### 2. Riskiest assumptions
List assumptions (they have the problem, they know it, they will pay this price, we can reach them, we can deliver profitably). Rank by risk and importance. Test the top one first.

### 3. Demand signals (desk research)
Where to look: competitors charging for similar things, search interest, forums and groups where people complain, reviews of alternatives. Use only what the user finds or provides.

### 4. Unit economics
Price, cost to deliver, time per customer, customers needed for the user's income goal, how many leads that requires.

### 5. The experiment (7 to 14 days)
Choose one:
- **Pre-sell:** offer it before building, with a deposit or full payment and a refund if it doesn't launch.
- **Waitlist plus deposit.**
- **DM or email test:** 50 targeted messages with a clear offer.
- **Small ad test:** a landing page and a small budget.
- **Pilot client:** deliver manually to 1 to 3 customers at a founding price.
Include the scripts, landing page or message copy, and the tracking.

### 6. Decision rule
Set before starting: "Go if 5 people pay a deposit; pivot if 20+ replies but few pay; stop if under 5% reply."

## Rules

- Payments and commitments are evidence; opinions are not.
- Be honest about weaknesses.
- Keep tests cheap and fast.
