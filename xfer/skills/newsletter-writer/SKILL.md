# Newsletter Writer

A newsletter earns its place in the inbox by being worth opening every time. One idea, delivered with personality and something the reader can use today, beats a long list of links. The subject line gets the open; the first line keeps them reading.

## What to collect

1. The newsletter's audience and promise (why people subscribed).
2. This issue's topic, story or news.
3. The call to action (reply, read, book, buy, share).
4. The writer's voice and recurring sections if any.
5. Length preference.

## The method

### 1. Subject line and preview
5 options. Curiosity, benefit or story. Under 50 characters. Preview text completes the thought.

### 2. Opening
A personal moment, a surprising fact, or a direct question. 2 to 4 short lines. No "Hope you're well".

### 3. The core idea
- One lesson or insight.
- Taught through a story, example, case study or mistake.
- Concrete steps or a framework.

### 4. Takeaways
3 bullets or a small checklist the reader can use today.

### 5. Optional sections
- Quick wins or tools (2 to 3).
- Something the writer is reading, watching or testing.
- Reader question or win.

### 6. Call to action
One clear action. For sales: a short, natural bridge from the idea to the offer.

### 7. Sign off and P.S.
Personal sign off. P.S. for a second hook or reminder.

### 8. Repeatable format
Suggest a template name and section order the user can reuse every week.

## Rules

- One idea per issue.
- Write to one reader.
- Short paragraphs, plenty of white space.
- Honest claims only. Links only where they add value.

## Output format

```
SUBJECT OPTIONS
...
PREVIEW TEXT
...

ISSUE
...

REUSABLE FORMAT
...
```

## Final check

- [ ] One idea
- [ ] Opening hooks in 3 lines
- [ ] Practical takeaways
- [ ] One CTA
