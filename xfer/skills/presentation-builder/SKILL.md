# Presentation Builder

A presentation is a story with evidence, not a document on a screen. Each slide makes one point, stated in its title. The audience should be able to read only the titles and understand the argument.

## What to collect

1. Audience, their knowledge level and what they care about.
2. The goal: what should they think, feel or do after.
3. Time available and format (in person, video, sent as a file).
4. Content: data, results, story, offer, notes.
5. Brand guidelines if any.

## The method

### 1. The key message
One sentence the audience should remember.

### 2. The storyline
Choose an arc:
- **Problem, solution, proof, plan, ask** (pitches).
- **Situation, complication, resolution** (reports, recommendations).
- **Before, turning point, after** (stories, case studies).
- **Teach: why, what, how, what if** (workshops).

### 3. Slide by slide
For each slide:
- **Title:** a full sentence stating the point ("Weekday bookings dropped 40% since March").
- **Content:** 3 bullets maximum, or one chart, or one image.
- **Visual:** chart type, image, diagram suggestion.
- **Speaker notes:** what to say, 30 to 90 seconds.
Typical length: 1 slide per 1 to 2 minutes.

### 4. Opening and close
Opening: a question, story, surprising number or the promise. Close: recap the key message and the specific ask or next step.

### 5. Questions
8 likely questions with short answers. Backup slides for detail.

### 6. Pitch deck variant (investors)
Problem, solution, market, product, traction, business model, competition, team, financials, ask.

## Rules

- One idea per slide.
- No paragraphs on slides.
- Charts labelled clearly, one insight highlighted.
- Real data only; mark placeholders.

## Output format

```
KEY MESSAGE
...
STORYLINE
...
SLIDES
1. Title: ...
   Content: ...
   Visual: ...
   Notes: ...
...
LIKELY QUESTIONS
...
BACKUP SLIDES
...
```

## Final check

- [ ] Titles tell the story alone
- [ ] One idea per slide
- [ ] Clear opening and ask
- [ ] Fits the time
