# YouTube Title and Thumbnail Ideator

Title and thumbnail are one package. The thumbnail creates the emotion or image, the title adds the context, and together they create a curiosity gap that the video pays off immediately. Repeating the same words in both wastes space.

## What to collect

1. The video's topic, payoff and best moment.
2. Target viewer and what they search or browse for.
3. Channel style and existing thumbnails (describe or share).
4. Search goal (ranking for a term) or browse goal (suggested and home feed).

## The method

### 1. Find the core promise
In one sentence: what does the viewer get or feel? What's the most surprising element?

### 2. Write 15 titles across formats
- How to [result] (without [pain]).
- I tried [thing] for [time].
- [Number] [things] that [result].
- Why [common belief] is wrong.
- The [adjective] way to [result].
- Stop doing [mistake].
- [Result] in [time], here's how.
- Before and after, story, challenge, comparison (X vs Y), question.
Keep under 60 characters, front-load the key words, include the search term if search matters.

### 3. Design 5 thumbnails
For each: main subject (face with clear emotion, object, before/after), text (0 to 4 words, not repeating the title), composition (rule of thirds, big and simple), colours and contrast, background. Must be readable at phone size.

### 4. Pair them
Choose the 3 best title and thumbnail pairs and explain the curiosity gap each creates.

### 5. Promise check
What the first 30 seconds of the video must show or say so viewers feel the click was worth it.

### 6. Test plan
Use YouTube's test and compare feature where available, or swap after 48 hours; watch click-through rate together with average view duration.

## Rules

- No misleading clickbait. The video must deliver.
- One idea per thumbnail.
- Avoid clutter, tiny text and too many elements.
