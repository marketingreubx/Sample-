# Project Brief Writer

Most bad work comes from a bad brief. A clear brief means fewer revisions, faster delivery and a happier client. This skill pulls the important information out of messy conversations and flags what is still missing.

## What to collect

1. The raw material: call notes, transcript, emails, WhatsApp messages, voice note text.
2. Who the brief is for (the user, a subcontractor, a team).
3. Type of project (design, content, website, video, campaign, ads).

## The brief structure

1. **Project name and owner.**
2. **Background:** the business and why this project exists now.
3. **Objective:** one sentence, measurable if possible.
4. **Audience:** who it is for, what they think and feel now, what we want them to think, feel and do.
5. **Key message:** the one thing to communicate.
6. **Supporting points:** proof, features, reasons to believe.
7. **Deliverables:** list with formats, sizes, lengths, quantities, platforms.
8. **Tone and style:** 3 adjectives, references (links), what to avoid.
9. **Mandatories:** logos, legal lines, hashtags, brand colours, fonts.
10. **No-gos:** things not to say or show.
11. **Budget and resources.**
12. **Timeline:** milestones, review dates, final deadline.
13. **Approvals:** who signs off.
14. **Success measures.**
15. **Open questions:** anything unclear or contradictory, with who must answer.

## Rules

- Use the client's words for objectives and audience where possible.
- Never fill gaps with assumptions without marking them [ASSUMPTION].
- Flag contradictions between sources.
- One page if possible. Bullets over paragraphs.
- For a subcontractor, remove sensitive client information that they do not need (prices, contracts).

## Output format

```
PROJECT BRIEF: [Name]
Owner ... | Date ... | For ...

1. Background
...
15. Open questions
- ... (ask: [who])

MISSING INFORMATION
...
CONTRADICTIONS FOUND
...
```

## Final check

- [ ] One clear objective
- [ ] Deliverables with specs
- [ ] Assumptions marked
- [ ] Open questions listed with owners
