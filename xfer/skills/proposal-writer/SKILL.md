# Proposal Writer

A proposal should feel like the client wrote the first half and the freelancer wrote the second. The first half proves you understood them (their situation, goals, what it costs to stay stuck). The second half shows the plan and makes saying yes easy. It should never be the first time the client hears the price.

## What to collect

1. Call notes or the client's brief: situation, problems, goals, budget hints, timeline, decision makers, words they used.
2. The user's service, process, proof and prices.
3. Any must-haves or deadlines.
4. Brand details (name, logo note, colours) if the user wants design notes.

## Proposal structure

1. **Cover:** "[Result] for [Client]", date, prepared by.
2. **Where you are now:** their situation in their words, the problems, what it costs them.
3. **Where you want to be:** goals and how success will be measured (numbers and dates where possible).
4. **The approach:** the plan in 3 to 5 phases, each with the why.
5. **Scope and deliverables:** exact items, quantities, frequency. **Not included** list.
6. **Timeline:** start date, milestones, review points.
7. **Why us:** relevant results, a short case study, testimonial.
8. **Investment:** three options (good, better, best) with the recommended one marked. Each lists the outcome and inclusions.
9. **Terms:** payment schedule, revisions, communication, client responsibilities, cancellation.
10. **Next steps:** how to accept (sign, pay deposit, book kickoff), and an expiry date for the proposal.

## Writing rules

- Lead with their goals, not your biography.
- Use their exact words from the call.
- One page summary at the top for busy readers.
- Plain language, short sentences, scannable.
- Only real proof. Placeholders in [brackets] for anything missing.
- Expiry date (for example 14 days) to encourage a decision, stated politely.

## The sending email

Short: recap the goal, mention the recommended option, invite questions, propose a 15 minute walk-through call. Never just attach and disappear.

## Follow up

Day 2: check they received it and offer to walk through it. Day 5: share a relevant result or idea. Day 10: polite nudge before the expiry date.

## Final check

- [ ] Opens with the client's situation and goals
- [ ] Success measures are concrete
- [ ] Scope has a not-included list
- [ ] Three options, one recommended
- [ ] Clear acceptance step and expiry
