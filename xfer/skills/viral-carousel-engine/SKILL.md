# Viral Carousel Engine

Carousels spread when a reader hits slide 2 and thinks "I did not know that, and I need it." That feeling comes from insider knowledge, not from organized information. Everything in this skill serves that one reaction.

---

## RULE ZERO: NO LISTICLES

A listicle is any carousel whose slides are interchangeable items under a numbered heading. "5 Ways To Grow", "7 Tools You Need", "10 Mistakes To Avoid". They are the default output of every AI and every low effort creator, so they read as generic before anyone reads a word.

**The test:** if you could reorder the middle slides without breaking the logic, it is a listicle. Rebuild it.

**Every carousel must have sequential dependency.** Slide 5 only makes sense because slide 4 happened. The reader swipes because the argument is unfinished, not because there are more items in a list.

The seven approved architectures are below. None of them are lists.

---

## RULE ONE: NO EM DASHES, ANYWHERE

Never use the em dash ( — ) or the en dash ( – ) in any slide, caption, headline, or line of output.

Use a period, a comma, a colon, parentheses, or restructure the sentence. Two shorter sentences almost always beat one sentence with a dash in it.

**Before delivering, scan your own output for these two characters. If either appears, rewrite that line.** This is the single most recognizable AI signature in short form copy and it disqualifies everything around it.

---

## RULE TWO: VALUE THAT IS NOT GOOGLEABLE

If someone could get the same answer from the first search result, the carousel has no reason to exist and will not be saved.

**Googleable value (banned):** definitions, generic best practices, tool lists, "why X matters", surface level explanations, anything a beginner article would say.

**Real value (required):** things learned by doing the work.

| Googleable slop | Real value |
|---|---|
| "Engagement matters for reach" | "Comments in the first 40 minutes moved my reach more than anything else I tested. Saves did almost nothing." |
| "Price your services confidently" | "I stopped sending prices in the DM. I say the number on the call instead. Close rate went from about 1 in 8 to 1 in 3." |
| "Use a content calendar" | "I batch on Sunday in one 90 minute block. Four videos, one carousel. Anything I cannot film in that block does not get made." |
| "Follow up with leads" | "Day 2, day 5, day 12. Day 12 is where most of my closes land, and almost nobody follows up that far." |

**Five sources of real value, strongest first:**
1. A specific number from your own work, with the context around it
2. An exact script, message, or phrase the reader can copy today
3. A named mistake, what it cost, and the change that fixed it
4. A sequence with real timing attached, not vague stages
5. A counterintuitive finding with the reasoning shown

**Hard requirement:** every value slide contains at least one of those five. A slide that contains none of them is filler and gets cut.

**If the user has not supplied real experience or numbers,** ask one focused question to get it. Do not invent a number, a client result, or a case study. Fabricated specifics are worse than vague copy because they get quoted back.

## THE SEVEN ARCHITECTURES

Pick the one that fits the material. Never default to the same one twice in a row.

**1. The Teardown.** Take one real example (a DM, a page, a post, a price sheet) and dissect why it worked or failed, line by line. Slides move through the example in order.

**2. The Reversal.** Name the accepted belief, show the evidence against it, replace it with what is actually true. Slides build the case.

**3. The Receipt.** Open with a real outcome, then walk backward through what produced it. Each slide is the previous decision.

**4. The Autopsy.** A failure, what it actually cost, the diagnosis, the change, the result now. Highest trust and highest reach of any format.

**5. The Walkthrough.** One process, shown in the order it happens, where each step depends on the last. Not a list of tips. A sequence that breaks if reordered.

**6. The Comparison.** Two real approaches run against each other with actual outcomes attached. Ends with the condition that decides which one applies.

**7. The Insider.** What people who do this work know that outsiders do not. Each slide reveals a layer deeper than the one before.

## SLIDE SPECS

**7 slides by default.** Range 6 to 9 when the argument genuinely needs it.

| Slide | Headline | Body |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (cover) | 6 to 12 words | 0 to 10 words |
| Middle slides | 4 to 8 words | 25 to 45 words |
| Final (CTA) | 5 to 10 words | 15 to 25 words |

Body over 45 words means split the slide or cut it. Nobody reads a paragraph on a phone.

**Rules for every slide:**
- One idea per slide
- Sentences under 15 words
- Contractions throughout
- Headline shapes must vary. If four headlines share a grammatical pattern, rewrite them.
- No slide may restate the cover
- The final slide is the only place you sell

---

## THE COVER

The cover does 90 percent of the work. It must stop a scroll with no context.

**Never open with a number and a plural noun.** "5 Ways", "7 Tools", "10 Mistakes", "3 Reasons". This is the exact pattern the user wants gone, and it signals listicle instantly.

**Open with one of these five instead:**

**Power statement.** A flat, confident claim that takes a side.
> "Posting every day is why your reach dropped."
> "Your prices are not the reason people say no."

**Hard statistic.** A real number with tension in it. Yours, or a cited one. Never invented.
> "I sent 400 cold DMs. 11 replied. All 11 had the same first line."
> "Nine of my last ten clients came from the same 4 minute call."

**Confession.** A specific mistake with a cost attached.
> "I lost a 3k client over one sentence in my onboarding."

**Contradiction.** Two true things that should not both be true.
> "My worst performing post brought in my biggest client."

**Direct callout.** Name the exact person and their exact situation.
> "If you are still charging per post, you are capped at 800 dollar clients."

**Cover rules:** under 12 words. Contains something specific. Makes a claim the carousel actually delivers. No "swipe to find out", no "you won't believe", no question marks as the whole hook.

---

## SLIDE 2 IS WHERE CAROUSELS DIE

Most people write a strong cover and a weak slide 2, so the swipe stops there.

**Slide 2 must widen the gap the cover opened.** Give the reader proof the cover claim is real, or raise the stakes, or reveal the first piece of the answer. Never use slide 2 to restate the cover or to introduce yourself.

Strongest slide 2 moves: the number behind the claim, the moment it happened, or the reason the common approach fails.

## THE AI KILL LIST

Any of these appearing in output means rewrite that line.

**Banned punctuation:** em dash ( — ), en dash ( – ), semicolons, ellipses used for suspense, emoji bullet points.

**Banned words:** delve, dive, unlock, unleash, elevate, harness, leverage, navigate, realm, landscape, tapestry, testament, journey, game changer, powerhouse, supercharge, skyrocket, transform, level up, crush it, dominate, secret sauce, blueprint, framework (as filler), robust, holistic, seamless, curated, actionable, insights, empower, foster, pivotal, crucial, vital, essential.

**Banned phrases:** here's the thing, the truth is, let that sink in, at the end of the day, in today's world, more than ever, sound familiar, what if I told you, imagine waking up, stop scrolling, you're leaving money on the table, work smarter not harder, the best part, here's why, let me explain, but here's where it gets interesting, this changed everything, and that's when it hit me, spoiler alert, plot twist, read that again.

**Banned sentence patterns:**
- "It's not just X. It's Y."
- "This isn't about X. It's about Y."
- "Not X, but Y."
- Colon reveal: "The result: something."
- Rhetorical question followed immediately by its own answer
- One word paragraph for drama. ("Nothing." "Exactly." "Silence.")
- Three items of near identical length in a row
- Opening a sentence with Ultimately, Notably, Importantly, Simply put, or In essence
- Closing slide that summarizes what the reader just read
- Any sentence that could be rewritten shorter without losing meaning

**Banned rhythms:**
- Every sentence roughly the same length
- Every slide roughly the same length
- Every headline the same grammatical shape
- Alternating short sentence, long sentence, short sentence in a visible pattern
- Perfectly balanced parallel construction across slides

**The rhythm requirement:** real writing is uneven. Some slides run 28 words, some run 44. Some sentences are four words. Some run nineteen. The unevenness is the human signal. If your slides are metronomic, break them on purpose.

---

## VOICE

Write the way someone talks when they know the subject cold and are not performing.

- Contractions always
- Second person singular. "You", never "you guys" or "everyone"
- State the point first, explain second. No build up on a slide.
- Opinions over observations. Take a side.
- Concrete nouns over abstract ones. "A 3k retainer" over "premium pricing."
- No hype adjectives. Cut incredible, powerful, amazing, insane, massive.
- Say the number. Say the name. Say the exact words used.
- If a line sounds like it belongs in a LinkedIn post, delete it.

---

## THE CAPTION

Every carousel ships with a caption.

- 70 to 130 words
- First line under 125 characters, works alone, does not repeat the cover
- Adds what the slides could not fit: the story behind it, a caveat, a personal detail
- One or two SEO keywords in the first two lines, placed naturally
- One CTA in the final line, matching the CTA slide but worded differently
- Exactly 3 hashtags, all lowercase, on the last line. One broad, one niche, one community or buyer intent.
- Same kill list applies. No em dashes in the caption either.

---

## PROCESS

1. Find the single sharpest idea in the topic. If the user gave a broad topic, narrow it to one claim.
2. Check for real value. If you have no number, script, mistake, sequence, or counterintuitive finding, ask the user one question to get one.
3. Pick an architecture that fits the material.
4. Write the cover three ways using three different hook types. Keep the one that would stop your own scroll.
5. Write slide 2 to widen the gap.
6. Build the middle slides with sequential dependency. Test by reordering them. If it still works, rewrite.
7. Write the CTA slide. One ask, with a reason.
8. Write the caption.
9. Run the audit below. Fix every failure. Then deliver.

---

## SELF AUDIT, RUN SILENTLY BEFORE EVERY DELIVERY

**Structure**
- [ ] Zero em dashes and en dashes in slides and caption. Scanned the actual characters.
- [ ] Not a listicle. Middle slides cannot be reordered without breaking.
- [ ] Cover does not open with a number plus plural noun.
- [ ] Cover under 12 words, specific, works with no context.
- [ ] Slide 2 widens the gap rather than restating.
- [ ] Every slide inside its word limit.
- [ ] Headline shapes vary across slides.
- [ ] Final slide is the only sell.

**Value**
- [ ] Every value slide has a number, script, named mistake, timed sequence, or counterintuitive finding.
- [ ] Nothing in here is available from a first page search result.
- [ ] Zero invented numbers, clients, or results.
- [ ] A reader could act on this differently tomorrow.

**Voice**
- [ ] Zero kill list words, phrases, patterns.
- [ ] Sentence lengths uneven. Slide lengths uneven.
- [ ] Contractions throughout.
- [ ] No closing summary slide.
- [ ] Would this read as human to a stranger with no context.

**Caption**
- [ ] 70 to 130 words, first line under 125 characters, does not repeat the cover.
- [ ] Exactly 3 lowercase hashtags on the final line.

## OUTPUT FORMAT

```
SLIDE 1
[Headline]
[Body if any]

SLIDE 2
[Headline]
[Body]

... through the final slide ...

CAPTION

[Caption text]

[3 lowercase hashtags]
```

Deliver that and nothing else. No architecture label, no strategy notes, no word counts, no explanation of choices, no "here is your carousel". If the user asks why, then explain.

If real value was missing and you had to leave a gap, add one closing line: `Needs from you: [the specific thing].`

---

## EDGE CASES

**Topic is thin.** Say so in one line, propose a sharper angle, then write that instead of padding seven slides with air.

**Topic is too big.** Narrow to one claim and name what you cut, so the rest becomes the next carousel.

**User gives a personal story or a real number.** Build the whole carousel around it. This is the strongest material that exists and it beats any framework.

**User asks for a listicle explicitly.** Give them the sequential version instead and say in one line why it will outperform. If they insist after that, follow the instruction.

**User supplies their own draft.** Keep their voice, their phrasing, their specifics. Fix only kill list violations, listicle structure, and spec breaks. Do not replace their personality with cleaner copy.

**Selling in the carousel.** Teach across every middle slide. Sell only on the last one. A pitch in the middle kills the swipe.

**User wants design direction.** Give it after the copy, in three lines maximum.
