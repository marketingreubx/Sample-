# Cold DM Personalizer

Templates fail because prospects can smell them. This skill keeps what makes a script work (its order, its psychology, its ask) and replaces everything generic with details only this prospect would recognise. The test: if you swapped the prospect's name for another business, the message should stop making sense.

## What to collect

1. **The script or template** (or the approach the user wants: problem spotted, compliment and audit, value first, soft question, direct offer, result for a peer).
2. **Prospect facts:** name, handle, business, what they sell, location, and at least one specific observation (a post, a gap, a win, a review, their bio, their booking link).
3. **The sender:** what they do, who they help, the result, one true proof point if any.
4. **Platform:** Instagram, WhatsApp, LinkedIn, TikTok, X, Facebook. Each has its own norms.

If there is no specific observation, ask for one or ask the user to paste their bio and last 3 captions. Do not personalise with guesses.

## The method

### 1. Break the template into its jobs
Label each line of the original: opener, rapport, observation, credibility, value, ask. Keep the jobs and their order. Throw away the wording.

### 2. Find the personal hooks
From the prospect facts pick:
- **One sincere, specific compliment** (a post, a result, the way they explain something). Never "love your page".
- **One observation** that connects to what the sender fixes, written to them: "your last 3 Reels get saves but your bio has no way to book".
- **One detail that proves a human looked:** a product name, a city, a recent post topic, a review quote.

### 3. Rewrite as a short sequence
- **DMs are texting, not letters.** 1 to 3 short messages, 1 to 3 sentences each. Send message 2 only after message 1 is delivered, not as one block.
- Message 1: name + specific compliment or context. No pitch.
- Message 2: the observation + why it matters + one line on what you do.
- Message 3: the easy ask (permission to send something, or a question).
- Fix grammar for the second person: "their website" becomes "your website", "they have" becomes "you have".

### 4. Adapt to the platform
- **Instagram/TikTok:** casual, one emoji maximum, no links in the first message (they get filtered).
- **WhatsApp:** greet first, mention how you found them, keep it short, never send voice notes cold.
- **LinkedIn:** slightly more formal, connection note under 300 characters, no pitch in the connection request.
- **X:** very short, reference their post directly.

### 5. Prepare the branches
Write a reply for "yes, send it", "what is it?", "how much?" and "not now", so the user is never stuck.

## Rules

- Keep the template's ask unless it is pushy. Upgrade "Can we hop on a call?" to "Can I send you 2 quick fixes?" when the relationship is cold.
- Never invent results, mutual connections or that you are a customer.
- No "I hope this finds you well", "I came across your profile", "quick question" as the only content.
- Never more than one question per message.
- Sound like the sender: mirror their tone if they gave samples.
- If the prospect posts in another language or dialect, match it.

## Output format

```
WHY THIS WILL FEEL PERSONAL
- Compliment used: ...
- Observation used: ...
- Detail that proves you looked: ...

MESSAGE 1 (send now)
...

MESSAGE 2 (send right after it is delivered)
...

MESSAGE 3 (send after they reply, or 1 day later)
...

IF THEY REPLY
- "Yes, send it": ...
- "What is it?": ...
- "How much?": ...
- "Not right now": ...

BEFORE YOU SEND
- Like or comment on one recent post first.
- Check [bracketed] details are true.
```

## Example

Original line: "Hey {name}, love your content! I help businesses grow on social media. Interested?"

Personalised:
1. "Hi Tolu, your 'before and after' carousel on hyperpigmentation was so clear, I saved it."
2. "One thing I noticed: people ask how to book in the comments but your bio link goes to your old Linktree with no booking page. I help skin clinics turn those comments into booked appointments."
3. "Want me to send a 60 second video showing a simple fix?"

## Final check

- [ ] Swap test: would this message break if sent to someone else?
- [ ] Short enough to read in one glance per message
- [ ] Second person grammar is correct
- [ ] One ask, low pressure, easy to say yes to
- [ ] Branch replies ready
- [ ] No invented facts, no em dashes
