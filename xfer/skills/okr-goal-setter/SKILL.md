# OKR Goal Setter

Goals fail when they are vague ("grow my business"), unmeasurable, or disconnected from weekly actions. OKRs fix that: one inspiring objective, a few measurable results, and the actions that drive them, reviewed every week.

## What to collect

1. The ambition and why it matters.
2. Time frame (90 days is ideal).
3. Current numbers (income, clients, followers, leads, conversion rates).
4. Available time, budget and help.
5. What has stopped them before.

## The method

### 1. Objective
One qualitative, motivating sentence: "Become the go-to social media manager for skincare clinics in Lagos."

### 2. Key results (3 to 4)
Measurable outcomes with a start and target: "Grow monthly retainer income from 400,000 to 1,200,000", "Sign 4 new retainer clients", "Book 20 discovery calls".

### 3. Leading indicators
The weekly inputs that predict the results: outreach messages sent, posts published, calls held, proposals sent.

### 4. Reality check
Work backwards with conversion assumptions: to sign 4 clients at a 25% close rate you need 16 calls; at 15% call rate from replies and 15% reply rate... show the math. Adjust targets if the inputs don't fit the available time.

### 5. Milestones
Month 1, 2, 3 targets.

### 6. Weekly actions
A recurring weekly checklist.

### 7. Risks and backup plans
Top 3 risks with if-then plans.

### 8. Tracking and review
Simple tracker (spreadsheet columns), weekly 15 minute check, monthly review, end-of-quarter scoring (0.0 to 1.0).

## Rules

- 3 to 4 key results maximum.
- Results are outcomes, not tasks.
- Ambitious but grounded in the math.

## Output format

```
OBJECTIVE
...
KEY RESULTS
1. From ... to ... by ...
LEADING INDICATORS (weekly)
...
THE MATH
...
MILESTONES
Month 1: ... Month 2: ... Month 3: ...
WEEKLY ACTIONS
- [ ] ...
RISKS AND BACKUPS
...
TRACKER AND REVIEW
...
```

## Final check

- [ ] Key results measurable with start and target
- [ ] Math shows inputs fit available time
- [ ] Weekly actions defined
- [ ] Review rhythm set
