# Contract in Plain English

Contracts are written to be precise, not readable. This skill translates each part into everyday language, highlights what could hurt the user, and gives them fair alternatives and questions, so they can sign confidently or negotiate. It is not legal advice, and it says so.

## What to collect

1. The contract text (or the relevant clauses).
2. Which side the user is on (freelancer, client, employee, partner).
3. The project or relationship and its value.
4. Country or state, if known (laws differ).
5. Specific worries.

## The method

### 1. One paragraph summary
What the agreement is, who it binds, what each side must do, how long it lasts, how it ends.

### 2. Clause by clause
For each clause: plain English meaning, what it means for the user in practice, and a rating:
- **Green:** standard and fair.
- **Amber:** common but worth checking or negotiating.
- **Red:** unusual, one-sided or risky.

### 3. Watch list (check these always)
Payment terms and late payment, scope and changes, revisions, intellectual property and when ownership transfers, usage rights and portfolio permission, confidentiality, non-compete and non-solicit, termination and notice, kill fees, liability caps, indemnities, warranties, dispute resolution and governing law, auto-renewal, exclusivity, chargebacks and refunds.

### 4. Suggested changes
For each amber or red clause, a fairer rewrite and a short, polite message to request the change.

### 5. Questions to ask before signing
5 to 10 clear questions.

### 6. Missing protections
What a fair agreement would normally include that this one does not.

## Rules

- Always state: "This is an explanation, not legal advice. For high-value or high-risk agreements, have a qualified lawyer review it."
- Do not invent clauses. Quote the exact wording when explaining.
- Neutral and fair. Point out terms that protect the user too.
- If something is ambiguous, say it is ambiguous.

## Output format

```
SUMMARY
...

CLAUSE BY CLAUSE
| Clause | Plain English | What it means for you | Rating |

RED AND AMBER FLAGS
1. [Red] "quoted wording" Why: ... Suggested wording: ...

MISSING PROTECTIONS
...

QUESTIONS TO ASK
...

MESSAGE TO REQUEST CHANGES
...

Not legal advice: ...
```

- [ ] Every important clause explained with its exact wording
- [ ] Ratings given

- [ ] Legal disclaimer included
