# Client Onboarding Builder

The first 14 days decide whether a client stays for months or regrets buying. Great onboarding removes uncertainty: the client knows what happens next, what they need to provide, how to communicate, and they see an early win quickly.

## What to collect

1. The service, package and start date.
2. What the user needs from clients (access, brand assets, content, information).
3. Tools used (Google Drive, Notion, Trello, WhatsApp, Slack, email).
4. The user's working hours and response times.
5. The first deliverable and when it is due.

## The system

### 1. Welcome message (within 1 hour of payment)
Thank them, confirm what they bought, the start date, the next 3 steps with dates, and where to ask questions. Warm and confident.

### 2. What happens next (timeline)
Day 0 to day 14 in simple steps. Example: Day 0 welcome and form, Day 2 kickoff call, Day 5 strategy draft, Day 7 approval, Day 10 first content live, Day 14 first check-in.

### 3. Intake questionnaire
Grouped questions: business basics, goals and success measures, audience, offers and prices, brand voice and visuals, competitors and inspiration, past attempts, approvals and decision maker, preferences and no-go topics. 15 to 25 questions, each easy to answer.

### 4. Access and asset checklist
Accounts (with secure sharing advice: add as collaborator, never share passwords in chat if avoidable), logos, fonts, photos, testimonials, product lists, price lists.

### 5. Kickoff call agenda (45 minutes)
Introductions and goals, review of form answers, priorities for the first month, how communication works, approvals and feedback rules, questions, confirm next steps.

### 6. Communication rules
Channel, hours, response times, how to send feedback, emergencies, meeting rhythm.

### 7. Early win
One small visible improvement in the first week (optimise the bio, fix the booking link, a quick audit).

### 8. Day 14 check-in
Questions to ask, what to report, and how to reset expectations if needed.

## Rules

- Friendly but structured. The client should never wonder what is next.
- Ask only for what you need.
- Security: recommend password managers or collaborator access.
