# Client Boundary Setter

Boundaries are a service quality decision, not a personal one. When the rules are clear from day one and restated calmly, most clients respect them. This skill writes the policy and the exact words for each situation, so the user never has to improvise when stressed.

## What to collect

1. The user's working days and hours and time zone.
2. Channels they use and want clients to use.
3. Current problems (late messages, weekend requests, voice notes, scope creep, calls without notice, rushed deadlines).
4. Their package terms (revisions, turnaround, support).
5. The tone they want: warm, neutral, firm.

## The method

### 1. The policy (for onboarding)
- Working hours and days.
- Response times per channel (for example, within 1 business day by email, same day for urgent issues flagged as URGENT).
- Preferred channels and what each is for.
- What counts as urgent and how to flag it.
- Meeting booking rules (scheduling link, notice needed).
- Feedback rules (one consolidated list per round).
- Rush requests (fee and minimum notice).
- Holidays and time off notice.

### 2. Messages for each situation
- Setting expectations at the start.
- A message received after hours (auto-reply or next-morning reply).
- Weekend request.
- "Can we jump on a quick call?" without notice.
- Scattered feedback across channels.
- Rush deadline request.
- A client who keeps pushing after a reminder.
Each: kind acknowledgement, the boundary, the alternative, the next step.

### 3. Tools
Auto-reply text for email and WhatsApp Business, quick replies, scheduling link note, notification settings tips.

### 4. Escalation
What to do if boundaries keep being crossed: a calm review call, a written reminder of the agreement, a rush or extra support fee, or ending the contract politely.

## Rules

- Kind and firm, never defensive or sarcastic.
- Offer an alternative every time ("I can look at this first thing Monday").
- Consistency matters more than the exact rule.
- Do not apologise for having working hours.
