# Prospect Research Brief

Ten minutes of research turns a cold message into a relevant one. This skill reads what the user provides and produces a brief that is honest about what is visible, ranks problems by what they cost, and hands over the exact angle and first line to use.

## What to collect

1. The user's offer: what they do, for whom, the result.
2. The prospect's material: website text, bio, recent captions, screenshots, reviews, Google listing, ads, job posts.
3. The channel the user will contact them on.

Only analyse what is provided. If something important is missing (for example the booking page), say what to check.

## The method

### 1. Snapshot
Who they are, what they sell, price signals, location, audience, stage (new, growing, established), how active they are.

### 2. How they get customers
Their funnel as far as visible: where attention comes from, where it lands, how someone books or buys, what happens after.

### 3. Visible problems (ranked by cost)
For each: what you saw (quote or describe it), why it costs them clients or money, how sure you are (Seen, Likely, Guess). Typical areas: unclear bio or headline, no clear call to action, broken or hidden booking, slow or not mobile friendly site, inconsistent posting, low engagement relative to followers, no reviews or proof, weak photos, no offer, ignored comments and DMs.

### 4. Opportunities the user can fix
Match the top problems to the user's service. Estimate impact in plain words, without inventing numbers.

### 5. The angle
- Best approach (problem spotted, compliment and audit, value first, soft question, result for a peer) and why.
- The observation sentence written to them: "I noticed your..."
- A genuine compliment.
- The opener (first message) and a follow up.

### 6. Call prep
Likely objections and how to answer, and 5 questions to ask if they agree to talk.

## Rules

- Never state a guess as fact. Label confidence.
- Be kind in wording. The prospect may read it someday.
- Do not suggest pointing out more than one problem in the first message.
- No invented numbers about their traffic or revenue.

## Output format

```
SNAPSHOT
...

HOW THEY GET CUSTOMERS
...

PROBLEMS (most costly first)
1. [Seen] ... Why it costs them: ...
2. ...

WHAT YOU CAN FIX
...

YOUR ANGLE
Approach: ... because ...
"I noticed your ..."
Compliment: ...
First message: ...
Follow up (day 2): ...

IF YOU GET A CALL
Objections: ...
Questions: ...
```

## Final check

- [ ] Every problem quotes or describes what was seen
- [ ] Problems ranked by cost
- [ ] One clear angle and a ready opener
- [ ] No guesses presented as facts
