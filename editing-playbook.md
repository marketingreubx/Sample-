# Reubx Short-Form Editing Playbook

Studied from 9 references (7 × @ricoincarnati, @benkimball.ai, @jumoke_xo) plus Reubx's own Cold DM Playbook reel.
Use this for every talking-head / tutorial edit.

## 1. Hook (first 0–2.5s)
- Big title over the speaker's chest, centred, two lines, heavy bold uppercase sans with a soft shadow:
  line 1 white, line 2 **yellow (#FFD600)**, optional small white line in brackets under it
  (e.g. `THE LAZIEST WAY` / `TO GO VIRAL` / `(S-tier Videos)`).
- Proof pops around the head: floating view-count badges (eye icon + "7.3M", one in red) or 2–3 tilted example
  thumbnails that pop in one by one with a soft "pop" each.
- Speaker is close and centred, eyes in the upper third. No slow intro: the first word is the hook.

## 2. Captions
- Small to medium, **white bold sans**, 1–4 words at a time, centred in the **middle of the frame** (about 55–62% down,
  over the chest), never at the very bottom.
- Soft drop shadow only: no thick outline, no boxes, no bouncing.
- Optional: active word or key word in the same yellow. Single-word captions work for fast sections.
- Reubx house variant: lowercase, neon-yellow active word, one italic serif accent word every few lines.

## 3. Colour grade (natural first)
- Skin stays true and warm and is never orange. Exposure lifted so the face is bright and clean.
- Contrast gentle. Background can be cooler or moodier (teal/blue room light) to separate the speaker.
- Saturation +5–10% at most. No heavy vignette, no crushed blacks on the face.
- One black-and-white punch-in is allowed on a dramatic line, as a moment, never a look.
- Always compare against the raw footage side by side: if it looks "graded", pull it back.

## 4. Pacing and motion
- Jump cut every 1.5–4s. Alternate wide and punch-in (1.0 ↔ 1.15). Hard punch-in (1.3+) on the punchline.
- Every pause and breath removed. Whole video 25–60s, 90s maximum for tutorials.

## 5. B-roll (the biggest difference between pro and bland)
- **Split layout:** B-roll in the TOP half (rounded corners, drop shadow), speaker keeps talking in the bottom half.
  The captions sit on the seam between them.
- B-roll types, in order of use:
  1. Screenshots of articles or tweets with **yellow highlighter** on the key phrase, plus a small source label.
  2. Comment screenshots and DM screenshots stacked as cards.
  3. Screen recordings framed with a **thin yellow glowing outline** on a dark background.
  4. Example posts and carousels with **red circles / red hand-drawn arrows** and yellow labels ("SLIDE 1", "FITNESS").
  5. Photos of the person being mentioned, popping in beside the head.
  6. Kinetic infographics (Ben Kimball): big counting numbers in green or yellow, terminal or app windows, icons on
     black, with the speaker in a rounded frame at the bottom.
- Full-screen B-roll sits on a dark background with **subtle red/orange light leaks, dust and grain**, never flat black.
- Animated helpers: countdown ring, progress dots, a tap cursor.

## 6. Transitions
- Mostly hard jump cuts. Transitions only at section changes: quick zoom-in/out (0.2–0.3s), whip/slide, light-leak flash.
- B-roll enters with a fast scale-pop (90%→100%) or slides up from below. It never just appears.

## 7. Sound
- Voice: noise reduction, de-ess, EQ (cut 200–300 Hz mud, +2–3 dB presence around 3 kHz), compression, -14 LUFS.
- Music: low background bed (about -25 dB under the voice), ducked when speaking, out cleanly at the end.
- SFX on every graphic: pop (badges, labels), whoosh (transitions, B-roll in), click / mouse (screen taps),
  camera shutter (screenshots), typing (text being typed), ding (wins, numbers). Quiet: felt, not heard.

## 8. Ending / CTA
- Cut the moment the CTA ends. CTA keyword on screen (comment bar typing the keyword, or bold text).

## 9. QA before delivery
- Face never covered, captions never on the mouth, nothing cut off at the edges.
- Grade side by side with the raw footage. Listen check: no clipped words at cuts.
- Check frames at every B-roll in and out, every transition, the hook and the CTA.

# Style B: "Floating-card editorial" (studied from @itsmegkilgore ×5 and @_anthonygallo)

Studied from 6 references: five reels by @itsmegkilgore (Reels Editing Engine launch: feature list, "dream out
loud", community, last-chance, laundry joke) and one 16:9 course promo by @_anthonygallo. The owner loves this style.
It is calmer and more premium than Style A (Enrico / Ben Kimball): the speaker barely moves, and the visual interest
comes from typography and floating UI cards, not from camera tricks.

## B1. Camera and framing
- One locked-off medium shot, eye level, the speaker centred and sitting low. **No punch-in zooms, no shake.**
  Cuts are rare and only change the angle:
  - a handheld selfie close-up for the hook and the CTA (the energy change signals "now listen")
  - a split screen for a gag
  - a picture-in-picture "me one month ago" phone frame (rounded corners, thin shadow) with a handwritten arrow
- Warm, natural indoor light kept as shot. Grade is only a light lift: no teal/orange, no crushed blacks, no vignette.
  Skin must stay true.

## B2. Captions: one word at a time, three typefaces
- **One word (sometimes 2-3) on screen at a time**, centred on the chest or just under the chin, about 55-62% down the
  frame. Never over the mouth. Each word is on screen for about 0.25-0.4s, so the text drives the pace.
- Base word: clean **white sans (Helvetica/Inter style), medium-bold, lowercase**, no outline, a very soft drop shadow.
- Emphasis word: **elegant serif italic in butter yellow (#F2E27A-ish)**, one size bigger (examples: *looks*, *autopilot*,
  *reel*, *unmistakably*, *decade*, *build*). About 1 in 4 words, always the meaningful noun/verb, never filler.
- Annotation line: a **handwritten marker font in pale yellow** for asides and sub-lines, small, often tilted,
  placed above her head or under the main text ("and not my creative voice.", "AI doesn't replace my ideas, it finally
  executes them.", "now over **800** people inside", "just wait for the ending").
- A typing caret appears on key lines as if the text were being typed ("you than|").
- Long thoughts can stack vertically as a word column (but / don't / just / build / an / offer / *build*), with the last
  word in yellow serif italic.

## B3. Hook (0-3s)
- A header block over the chest: small handwritten yellow line + big white sans line + handwritten sub-line, e.g.
  `15 ways` / `Claude edits my reels,` / `and not my creative voice.`
- Or a curiosity line in the same type system: "send this to a *dreamer*", with the sub-line "it's time to say it out loud".
- A small card or thumbnail pops in the corner right away so the eye has something to explore.

## B4. Floating UI cards (the signature move)
- Every spoken beat of a "list" reel gets a **white rounded card with a soft shadow** that pops in above or beside the
  head, mocking the thing being named, then leaves when the next beat starts (each lasts 1.5-3s):
  - Visual editor window / script card with a struck-through word and a scissors icon ("I can click any word and it cuts it")
  - "My lookbook" with 3 thumbnails; brand kit with colour dots + font samples (Aa)
  - GIF library grid with a meme popping out of it; music picker with a heart cursor
  - A pill reading "Autopilot" with a rocket emoji; a small phone-frame preview of a finished reel
  - Stacked, tilted comment/DM screenshots (names blurred) for social proof
  - Chat bubbles (blue/grey) in a split screen for a joke
- Emoji stickers (🤯 🚀) are allowed as accents. Cards are drawn in the brand colours and fonts.
- The speaker never gets covered: cards live in the top third and the top corners.

## B5. Motion, transitions, ending
- Words and cards **pop (scale 85%→105%→100% in 0.15s) or fade in with a slight blur**, then fade out. Nothing just appears.
- Hard cuts only, plus one handheld angle change into the CTA. No whooshes between every shot.
- **End card:** a plain black frame with serif italic text ("Doors close tonight / 7:00 pm central") and one
  handwritten line below ("P.S. thanks for changing…"). A CTA keyword is said and shown ("comment ENGINE").
- Length: feature/launch reels 30-65s; story reels 100s+ only when the story is the product.

## B6. Premium SaaS-promo variant (Anthony Gallo, 16:9)
- Light lavender/white backgrounds with soft gradients; clean motion-graphic cards: video timeline, an "Edit!" prompt
  bar, a "Content engine" brain-icon card, a "Plan. Script." flowchart, a leaderboard, a "30-day refund policy" badge.
- Big sans headlines with **gradient text** (blue→purple→orange) on one key phrase.
- Captions are white bold with a soft shadow in the lower third; the host is cut in between full-screen graphics.

## B7. Sound (what could be confirmed)
- The reels' audio is the speaker's own voice, close-mic'd and clean, with a quiet music bed and short UI sounds.
  The one reel with a mixed sound list used stock "Whoosh", "Typing", "Camera shutter", "Mouse click", "Pop",
  "Item pickup", "Ka-ching", "Coin collect". Keep every effect short and low.
- I could not hear the audio in this session; the above comes from the sound metadata and the visuals. Treat the
  loudness and music level as -14 LUFS with the bed about 20-25 dB under the voice.

## B8. When to choose which style
- **Style A** (Enrico / Ben Kimball): news, hot takes and tutorials where the proof is screenshots; higher energy.
- **Style B** (this section): product/feature lists, launches, personal-brand and story reels; calmer, premium.
- For Reubx's esthetician-agency content: Style B cards map well to a content calendar card, a DM-reply card,
  a pricing card, a before/after card and a booking-link pill.

## B9. Limits of this study
- Frames were sampled at about 200px wide, so font names are best matches (Inter/Helvetica sans, Instrument Serif or
  Playfair italic, a marker font like Caveat), not confirmed.
- I analysed scene structure for 3 of the 6 reels with a video-analysis tool; the other 3 were studied from frames only.

# Environment recipes (this cloud container; they do not carry over automatically)
- Instagram/TikTok CDN downloads are blocked from the shell. Workaround: run the Apify actor `tiger19935/video-frame-extractor`
  on the video URL (it can reach it) to get frames and contact sheets, and the Higgsfield `media_import_url` +
  `video_analysis_*` tools for scene-by-scene analysis.
- Stock photos: `memo23/pexels-scraper` finds them, but `images.pexels.com` is blocked; pass the image URL to the frame
  extractor (mode `timestamps`, `[0]`) to get a downloadable copy.
- Transcription: Hugging Face is blocked. Use sherpa-onnx with the Parakeet TDT int8 model from the GitHub release
  `asr-models/sherpa-onnx-nemo-parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v2-int8.tar.bz2` (60s chunks cut at the quietest point).
- Noise removal: `arnndn` needs an RNNoise model; `sh.rnnn` downloads from raw.githubusercontent.com (GregorR/rnnoise-models).
- Fonts that work in libass here: Poppins and Montserrat (skill assets), Inter (system), Instrument Serif Italic
  (raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/instrumentserif/). Copy them to `/root/.fonts` and run `fc-cache -f`.
- Face tracking for caption placement: `opencv-python-headless==4.10.0.84` (5.x has no CascadeClassifier) with the
  Haar cascade from raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/data/haarcascades.
- Rendering: a 1080x1920 full composite of a long clip runs out of memory in one ffmpeg pass; cut per segment, concat,
  then composite overlays in a second pass.
